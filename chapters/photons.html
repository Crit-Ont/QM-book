5  Appendix: Photons in the quantum substratum and spacetime

In PTQT the difference between real and virtual photons is explicitly defined by whether a complete, energy-conserving transaction is formed. Instead of treating both as different “types” of independent field excitations, PTQT views them as fundamentally distinct stages of relational interactions between charged particles.

##Real photons

A real photon in QFT is an on‑shell excitation of the electromagnetic field that can be detected, while a virtual photon is an off‑shell internal line in a perturbative calculation that never appears as an observable particle. A real photon satisfies the physical dispersion relation \(E^2 = p^2 c^2\) and can be emitted, absorbed, or counted. A virtual photon does not satisfy this relation; it is a mathematical intermediate in the perturbation expansion of the S‑matrix and cannot be directly observed.

Real photons (on‑shell) appear in the external legs of Feynman diagrams and are detectable: you can measure their energy, momentum, polarization. Their key features are

  • On‑shell: obey \(E^2 = p^2 c^2\).
  • Long‑lived: propagate freely.
  • Transfer energy–momentum: they mediate real radiation.
  • Create or annihilate excitations in detectors or atoms.

Real photons correspond to actual events — “something happened” in the experimental sense.

In PTQT a photon becomes “real” when an excited emitter successfully completes a “handshake” with a specific absorber rather than just a potential one.

  • The emitter state and the absorber states set up a set of possibilities.
  • If and only if the conservation laws are perfectly satisfied between those two specific matter states, a non-unitary collapse occurs, which is a stochastic transition from the possible to the actual. The energy is transferred, and a real photon is actualised.
  • While standard quantum physics defines a real photon as something that can theoretically travel infinitely through space without an absorber, in PTQT a real photon always has an absorber on the other end. It is bounded by its emission and absorption events.

5.1 Virtual photons

Virtual photons in QFT are internal lines in Feynman diagrams. They can be considered to be mathematical devices in the Dyson expansion of the S‑operator.

Their key features are:

  • Off‑shell: do not satisfy \(E^2 = p^2 c^2\).
  • Short‑lived: exist only within the integral over intermediate momenta.
  • Not observable: cannot be detected or counted.
  • Encode interactions: they represent how charged particles influence each other.

Virtual photons are not just part of the dressing of charged particles, where in standard quantum field theory they appear as aspects of the calculation, in PTQT they enable the transaction.

Only real photons correspond to measurable radiation and virtual photons are indispensable in calculations but their physical existence is in the quantum substratum. Confusing the two leads to misconceptions like “vacuum is full of particles popping in and out of existence.” In QFT, virtual particles are terms in an integral, not literal transient objects, but in PTQT they are essential for completing the transaction.

A virtual photon in the substratum enables the transaction between the Emitter States and the set of possible Absorber States. It does not select the specific absorber but does select a specific \(\sigma\)-algebra of possible absorbers. The selection of a specific absorber is a stochastic transition.

Ontological status of the virtual photon in the quantum substratum (PTQT):

  • It is not a particle and not a spacetime object.
  • It enables the transaction between the emitter and the selected absorber.
  • It exists only in the quantum substratum, which is not spacetime and has no metric, no geometry, no trajectories.

Ontological status of the real photon in spacetime (PTQT):

  • It is the actualised transfer of conserved quantities (energy–momentum).
  • It is a spacetime connection between an emission event and an absorption event.
  • Key properties:
    • Exists only after a transaction (NU‑process).
    • Defines spacetime events — absorption events become actual spacetime nodes.
    • Carries energy–momentum — unlike the transaction photon.
    • Has a spacetime trajectory (null geodesic) only once spacetime exists.
