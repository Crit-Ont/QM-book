4  Possibilist Transactional Quantum Theory (PTQT)

Ruth Kastner’s “Relativistic (Possibilist) Transactional Interpretation” (PTI) gives a very explicit and technically different answer to Bell locality than Cramer’s original Transactional Interpretation (TI). Her answer is grounded in direct‑action quantum field theory (DAQFT) and an ontology of real physical possibilities leading to spacetime events.

This chapter presents a concise conceptual summary of Possibilist Transactional Quantum Theory (PTQT), focusing on two questions:

  1. How PTQT can be reformulated within the probabilistic reconstruction of quantum theory (Chapter 1)
  2. How PTQT can be used to describe the ontological status of the quantum substratum.
  3. How reformulated PTQT addresses Bell locality and correlations without invoking spacetime superluminal causation.

To address the first point, a simple change will be made. All talk of waves will be eliminated. The immewdiate changes are:

  • Offer waves (OWs) will be replaced by emission states (ES) of the emitter.
  • Confirmation waves (CWs) will be replaced by absorber states (AS)

4.1 The quantum substratum

Kastner’s PTI is often described as an interpretation of quantum mechanics (Kastner (2022)). In this chapter, it is treated as a physical theory with explicit ontological commitments; for that reason, the name Possibilist Transactional Quantum Theory (PTQT) is used to reflect this status.

The core claim is that a quantum substratum of physically real possibilities underlies, and specifically causes, observable spacetime events. The theory addresses both the measurement problem and Bell-type correlations. Except for the ad hoc measurement step in standard quantum theory all quantum processes take place in the substratum. They are not spacetime processes. In PTQT, the substratum is a domain of potentiality rather than a collection of actual spacetime objects. It is not directly observable; its structure is inferred from the formalism and from the pattern of actualized events.

A potentiality-based reading of quantum theory has precedents (e.g., Heisenberg (1958)). PTQT extends this by tying potentiality to a transactional mechanism that yields concrete events. The substratum is an aspect of the inorganic layer in the Real Sphere. It is an extension of the more classical conception that is to be found in Hartmann’s writings. Quantum unitary dynamics is a process in the substratum, and the non-unitary actualisation of events is a process that creates events in spacetime. The spacetime events are also items in the inorganics and exist in the Real Sphere. The substrate exists as the domain of real possibilities and the spacetime is the domain of real events.

On this view, spacetime is not fundamental. Events may be ontologically prior, with spacetime emerging from relations among events. This reframes matter-like entities as emergent from an event-structured process rather than as fundamental substances.

PTQT describes actualisation as a stochastic selection among physically possible transactions:

  1. A relativistic quantum state evolves into a state supporting multiple possible transactions.
  2. The context defines transition amplitudes/probabilities over distinct possibilities.
  3. One possible transaction is actualized through stochastic selection.
  4. The outcome is a spacetime event and an updated set of future possibilities.

This actualisation process is non-unitary and stochastic and it is a process that creates events in spacetime. Measurement is modeled as selection among multiple incipient transactions, sometimes described as spontaneous symmetry breaking in the transaction set. The resulting event is an actualised outcome in spacetime, while unrealised alternatives remain non-actualised possibilities.

Measurement actualisation is spontaneous symmetry breaking In PTQT:

  • Multiple “incipient transactions” exist as possibilities.
  • A single transaction is actualised via spontaneous symmetry breaking according to Kastner but this is an analogical description. THere is no Higg’s mechanism in the substratum. The selection is stochastic and non-unitary.

This actualisation creates spacetime events. Bell correlations reflect constraints on which transactions can actualise, not causal influences between spacetime-separated detectors.

4.2 Time in PTQT

If time in the empirical sense is emergent then what is it that indexes the dynamics in the pre-spacetime quantum substrate? In the PTQT spacetime is not a fundamental container. Instead, physical spacetime and its temporal structure emerge from a deeper, extra-spatiotemporal realm called the quantum substratum (Kastner (2022)). The time mechanism in this framework operates through the following core concepts:

  • In PTQT, the quantum substratum consists of quantum states existing as potentiae (possibilities or unactualized offers and responses). Time does not flow smoothly as a background parameter in this substratum. Rather, spacetime events and their structural connections are established exclusively by “actualized transactions”.
    • When a quantum emitter sends an offer wave and an absorber responds, a transaction can become actualized (a process corresponding to objective wave function collapse).
    • An actualized transaction instantiates a discrete emission event, an absorption event, and the invariant spacetime interval connecting them. Spacetime effectively “crystallizes” out of these events.
  • PTQT rejects the “block universe” ontology where past, present, and future all exist simultaneously.
    • In the quantum substratum, the primary temporal feature is ontological change driven by the state transitions of interacting quantum systems.
    • The “Now” is the shifting domain of active actualization. The future does not exist yet because it consists only of unactualized possibilities within the substratum. A time index (\(t\)) is merely a relational way of indexing and mapping these changes after they have been archived into the emergent fabric of spacetime.
  • Standard quantum mechanics operates on time-symmetric laws, making it a mystery why time only moves forward. RTI solves this by introducing a physical mechanism for temporal symmetry breaking:
    • The unactualized offers and responses in the substratum are fundamentally time-symmetric.
    • However, the non-unitary phase where an absorber responds transforms the time-symmetric propagator into the time-asymmetric Feynman propagator (which propagates positive energy forward in time).
    • This actualization process inherently breaks time symmetry, generating a unidirectional arrow of time and grounding the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

In summary, normal time is relational and emergent: the quantum substratum itself is extra-spatiotemporal, while the linear flow of time we perceive is a macro-level byproduct of discrete, irreversible quantum transactions breaking symmetry at the micro-level.

As the quantum substratum is extra-spatiotemporal, how can we speak of “unitary evolution” (like the solution of the Heisenberg equation or quantum operators) which explicitly depends on a time parameter? Ruth Kastner resolves this by introducing a strict distinction between two fundamentally different types of time: Coordinate Time (which is emergent and relational) and Process Time (which is fundamental and non-spatial). Here is how unitary dynamics can function in a substratum without relying on physical spacetime:

4.2.1 Process Time vs. Coordinate Time

In PTQT, the time parameter (t) found in standard quantum mechanical equations does not refer to the ticking of a clock in physical space. Instead, it refers to Process Time:

  • Process Time: This is a sequential, logical ordering of changes in quantum states. It represents the generation of possibilities—offers being sent and responses being generated. It is a time of becoming, not a time of extension.
  • Coordinate Time: This is the time we measure with macro-clocks (e.g., \(x^0\) in relativity). It only exists after a transaction has collapsed the quantum state and created a physical event.

Therefore, unitary dynamics describes the evolution of possibilities in Process Time before they ever “solidify” into the Coordinate Time of our physical universe. Because the substratum lacks spatial distance, all quantum systems within it are fundamentally in a state of immediate, non-local availability to one another.Therefore: - Unitary evolution in this realm is not a movement through space over time. The time \(t\) in the unitary operator \(U(t)=e^{-iHt}\) acts as a relational index for these internal state changes, rather than a measure of duration through an external physical container.

4.3 Bell locality in PTQT

Bell locality (Chapter 3) applies only to statistics of spacetime events. Kastner argues that Bell’s locality condition applies only to actualized spacetime events in PTQT but are not relevant to the pre‑spacetime quantum substratum where emission states and absorber states exist.

In PTQT, the states involved in quantum processes are not spacetime objects; they are possibilities described by state space. Thus, Bell’s locality assumption does not apply at the level where entanglement correlations originate. That is in the quantum substratum. The Implication is that Bell’s theorem does not constrain PTQT because the correlations arise before spacetime events exist — in a domain where “Bell locality” is not defined.

PTQT’s response to Bell’s theorem is that Bell locality applies to actualized spacetime events, not to the pre-spacetime domain in which transactional possibilities are defined.

Emission states and absorber states are treated as elements of that possibilist structure, not as classical signals propagating through spacetime. Under this interpretation, Bell-violating correlations are generated by nonlocal constraints at the possibilist level, rather than by superluminal causal influence between detectors.

PTQT therefore accepts nonlocal correlations while aiming to preserve relativistic covariance: the key process is modeled as pre-spatiotemporal, avoiding a preferred foliation.

Kastner explicitly accepts nonlocal correlations, but denies that they imply spacetime nonlocal causation. The causal aspect belong to the substratum. In PTQT, the transaction between emitter and absorber is a pre‑spacetime process realised through the exchange of a virtula photon. It is not a signal traveling through spacetime, so it does not violate relativistic locality. The implication is that Bell violations arise from a global constraint in the quantum substratum, not from superluminal influences.

4.4 PTQT correlations

PTI uses direct‑action theory (DAQFT) to explain correlations and events without hidden variables. It is built on Davies’ quantum direct‑action theory, where interactions occur through:

  • Emission states (ES) - actually the quantum state of the emitter (ES) 1
  • Confirmation waves (CW) - actually the quantum state of the absorber (AS)

These are not fields propagating locally in spacetime but nonlocal interaction amplitudes. The coupling constant (charge) is interpreted as the amplitude for generating ES/AS.

Bell’s factorizability condition fails because the underlying ontology is nonlocal by construction, but not in a way that threatens relativity.

4.5 Retrocausality and atemporal constraint language

A recurring interpretive issue is whether PTQT should be described as retrocausal. A useful distinction is between:

  • Dynamical retrocausality (literal backward-in-time causal propagation), and
  • Atemporal/all-at-once constraint structure (global consistency conditions without propagating backward signals).

For PTQT, the second description is typically more precise: advanced mathematical components need not imply dynamical backward causation.

4.6 Photons in the quantum substratum spacetime

A real photon in QFT is an on‑shell excitation of the electromagnetic field that can be detected, while a virtual photon is an off‑shell internal line in a perturbative calculation that never appears as an observable particle. That is the essential distinction.

A real photon satisfies the physical dispersion relation \(E^2 = p^2 c^2\) and can be emitted, absorbed, or counted. A virtual photon does not satisfy this relation; it is a mathematical intermediate in the perturbation expansion of the S‑matrix and cannot be directly observed. Physics Forums

Real photons (on‑shell) are:

  • Actual quanta of the electromagnetic field.
  • Appear in the external legs of Feynman diagrams.
  • Are detectable: you can measure their energy, momentum, polarization.

Their key features are

  • On‑shell: obey \(E^2 = p^2 c^2\).
  • Long‑lived: propagate freely.
  • Transfer energy–momentum: they mediate real radiation.
  • Create or annihilate excitations in detectors or atoms.

Real photons correspond to actual events — “something happened” in the experimental sense. Springer

Virtual photons (off‑shell) are

  • Internal lines in Feynman diagrams.
  • Mathematical devices in the Dyson expansion of the S‑operator.
  • Never appear as asymptotic states.

Their key features are:

  • Off‑shell: do not satisfy \(E^2 = p^2 c^2\).
  • Short‑lived: exist only within the integral over intermediate momenta.
  • Not observable: cannot be detected or counted.
  • Encode interactions: they represent how charged particles influence each other.

Virtual photons are not just part of the dressing of charged particles, where in standard quantum field theory they appear as aspects of the calaculation. In PTQT they make the transaction.

4.7 Haag locality clash with PTQT

In axiomatic foundation for quantum theory as proposed in Haag (1996), Haag locality is a spacetime concept, and PTQT denies that spacetime is fundamental. In PTQT the operators act on pre-spacetime quantum states Therefore, Haag locality cannot be a fundamental axiom of quantum theory if PTQT is correct2.

4.7.1 Haag locality presupposes spacetime

Haag’s Local Quantum Physics (LQP) is built on three pillars:

  • Local operator algebras assigned to spacetime regions
  • Microcausality: operators in spacelike‑separated regions commute
  • Haag duality: the algebra of a region equals the commutant of its causal complement

All of these pillars require:

  • A background spacetime
  • A causal structure
  • Localizable observables
  • Region‑indexed operator algebras

So Haag locality is intrinsically spacetime‑dependent. It invokes nothing more fundamental than spacetime geometry. It is an axiomatisation of QFT on spacetime.

4.7.2 PTQT denies spacetime at the fundamental level

In Kastner’s PTQT emitter states (ES) and absorber states (AS) exist in a pre‑spacetime substratum

  • Incipient transactions are pre-spacetime possibilities. There is a substratum time but this a time intrinsic to the non-zero rest mass particles concerned in the transaction.
  • The combination of an ES with potential absorber states defines a set of possible transactions.
  • The combination of the ES and each AS defines a set of trasnsition probabilities for the possible transactions.
  • An event is actualized when one of the possible transactions is selected via a stochastic process. This is a spontaneous process that selects one transaction from the set of possibilities.
  • Actualisation creates a spacetime interval event.
  • Spacetime is not fundamental, but emergent from transactions
  • The emergent space-time is an attribute of the inorganic layer in a general ontology.

Therefore, Before actualisation, there are no spacetime regions, no causal complements, and no operator algebras indexed by regions. Haag locality simply has no domain of definition at the fundamental level. Therefore Haag locality cannot be fundamental. If spacetime is emergent then Haag locality is also emergent. It is a constraint on actualised events, not a fundamental axiom of the quantum substratum.

Thus Haag locality is at best an emergent constraint on actualised events, not a fundamental axiom of the quantum world. This means Haag’s framework cannot be the foundational axiomatic structure of quantum theory if PTQT is correct. It becomes a derived effective theory that is valid only after actualisation, not before. It is a phenomenological constraint on the emergent spacetime layer, not a fundamental axiom of the quantum substratum.

4.7.3 The consequence for axiomatic foundations

If PTQT is correct:

  • Haag locality cannot be a fundamental axiom
  • Haag–Kastler algebraic QFT cannot be the foundational ontology
  • Local operator algebras are emergent structures
  • The axioms of LQP describe only the emergent spacetime layer

This is analogous to:

  • Thermodynamics not being fundamental if statistical mechanics is correct
  • Continuum elasticity not being fundamental if atomic physics is correct

Haag locality becomes a phenomenological constraint on the emergent spacetime layer.

4.7.4 What an operator algebra is in Haag’s framework

In Haag’s Local Quantum Physics (LQP), the basic objects are:

  • C\(^*\)‑algebras or von Neumann algebras

  • indexed by spacetime regions \(\symcal{A}(\mathcal{O})\)

  • satisfying locality:

    \[ [\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}_1), \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}_2)] = 0 \quad \text{if } \mathcal{O}_1 \text{ and } \mathcal{O}_2 \text{ are spacelike separated} \]

Everything — locality, duality, isotony, covariance — depends on spacetime geometry.

Without spacetime, the indexing collapses and the axioms cannot even be stated.

4.7.5 PTQT’s quantum substratum has no spacetime

In Kastner’s PTQT:

  • Offer waves (OWs) and confirmation waves (CWs) exist in a pre‑spacetime substratum
  • Incipient transactions are atemporal possibilities
  • Actualisation (NU‑process) creates a spacetime interval
  • Before actualisation, there are no regions, no causal complements, no metric, no light cones.

Thus:

There is no structure on which a Haag–Kastler net of algebras could be defined.

The substratum is not a manifold, not a causal set, not a metric space, not a topological space.
It is a Hilbert‑space possibility structure, not a spacetime.

One might try to salvage locality by saying:

“Let’s index algebras by something other than spacetime — e.g., degrees of freedom, emitters, absorbers, or ES/AS channels.”

But this fails because the notion of locality exists in the substratum as locality is a relation between regions and the substratum has no regions. Therefore operator locality is undefined in the substratum. Not violated — simply meaningless.

4.7.5.1 What can exist in the substratum

PTQT does have algebraic structure, but it is:

  • global, not local
  • constraint‑based, not region‑based
  • possibility‑based, not event‑based
  • atemporal, not temporal
  • nonlocal in Bell’s sense, but non‑signalling

You can have:

  • Hilbert space
  • operators representing OWs and CWs
  • amplitudes
  • global constraints on entangled systems
  • Born‑rule weights for incipient transactions

But you cannot have:

  • local operator algebras
  • nets of algebras
  • microcausality
  • Haag duality
  • isotony
  • region‑indexed structures

Because all of these require spacetime, which PTQT says is not fundamental. If PTQT is correct, Haag locality is not fundamental and cannot serve as an axiomatic foundation for quantum theory. Haag locality becomes:

  • an emergent constraint
  • applicable only after actualisation
  • valid only within spacetime
  • not part of the fundamental ontology

4.7.5.2 Operators exist in the substratum — but they are not local operators

PTQT’s quantum substratum is:

  • pre‑spacetime
  • atemporal
  • nonlocal in Bell’s sense
  • a Hilbert‑space possibility structure

In this domain, there are:

  • Hilbert space
  • linear operators
  • self‑adjoint operators
  • projectors
  • unitary operators
  • tensor‑product structure
  • global constraints on entangled states

These operators form a perfectly good algebraic structure: a C*‑algebra or von Neumann algebra of operators on Hilbert space. This is the same algebraic backbone used in the probabilistic reconstruction of quantum mechanics (Chapter 1). PTQT supports a global operator algebra, not a local one. It is a Hilbert‑space operator algebra and the operators act on:

  • Emitter states
  • Absorber response states
  • entangled states
  • prepared states
  • global possibility structures

This is the usual algebra of quantum theory:

\[ \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}) \]

or a suitable C*‑subalgebra. It supports unitary evolution - the U‑process is governed by: \[ U(t) = e^{-iHt} \]

where \(t\) is Process Time (internal periodicity), not spacetime time.

  • It supports projectors
    • Incipient transactions correspond to projectors onto matched OW–CW subspaces.
  • It supports Born‑rule weights
    • The algebra supports the spectral decomposition needed for probabilities.
  • It supports global constraints
    • Entangled systems impose nonlocal constraints on the operator algebra.
  • It does not support locality
    • Because locality requires spacetime, which is emergent.

Once a transaction actualises:

  • a spacetime interval is created
  • emitter and absorber actualise spacetime events

At this stage quantum mechanics has done its job. There is no need for operators to be assigned to regions. Thus, local operator algebras are not even emergent structures let alone fundamental ones.

Operators in the PTQT substratum must have an algebraic structure — and they do.
But it is a global Hilbert‑space operator algebra, not a Haag–Kastler local algebra.

Therefore:

  • Global operator algebra: fundamental
  • Local operator algebra: not applicable
  • Haag locality: not applicable
  • PTQT substratum: algebraic but nonlocal
  • Spacetime locality: created by actualisation

This is exactly the distinction needed to reconcile PTQT with operator theory while rejecting Haag locality as a foundational axiom.

4.8 State preparation, spacetime and the quantum substratum”

State preparation has similarities with measurement. A measurement leaves the system under investigation in a certain state. State preparation in Kastner’s Relativistic Transactional Interpretation (PTQT) (Kastner (2022)) influences the quantum substratum by selecting and shaping the set of incipient transactions that exist as physically real possibilities prior to spacetime actualisation. It does so through isolating the system under investigation and then controlling its interactions with a preparatory absorber.

The setting up of the preparatory absorber takes place in spacetime, but the state preparation is not in spacetime; it is a constraint on the pre‑spacetime possibility structure from which actualised events emerge.

In PTQT, state preparation modifies the quantum substratum by altering the configuration of emission states of the system under investigation and absorber states that exist as pre‑spacetime possibilities. These prepared states determine which incipient transactions are available and therefore which spacetime events can emerge through the non‑unitary (NU) actualisation process.

Kastner describes the quantum substratum as a pre‑spacetime domain consisting of quantum systems with rest mass and internal periodicities that act as “internal clocks” and define inertial frames. The substratum contains in Kastner’s terminology:

  • Offer waves (OWs) generated by emitters
  • Confirmation waves (CWs) generated by absorbers
  • Incipient transactions: matched OW–CW pairs representing possible energy‑transferring events

These are representations of possibilities in Hilbert space.

The generalised probability reconstructions of quantum mechanics and now the enhanced PTQT show that the quantum substratum is a pre‑spacetime domain in which the possibilities for actualisation are constrained by the global structure of the system. State preparation is a process that constrains the substratum and thereby influences which spacetime events can emerge. There is no place other than historical respect for Schrodinger to talk about waves. So instead of describing the processes in terms of offer waves and confirmation waves, we can describe them in terms of the emission states (ES) of the system under investigation and the absorber states (AS).

State preparation—whether by measurement, filtering, or controlled interactions—changes the quantum state that undergoes unitary evolution (the U‑interaction).

Prepared states determine:

  • which ES exist,
  • their amplitudes,
  • their phase relations,
  • the set of potential absorbers,
  • and therefore the structure of incipient transactions.

Thus, state preparation reconfigures the landscape of possibilities in the substratum.

4.8.1 How state preparation influences the substratum

It changes the set of emission states by preparing a quantum state, which changes the ES of a system. Since ES are the “invitations to transact,” modifying them directly alters the substratum’s possibility structure. It changes which absorber states can respond to the emission states, and therefore which incipient transactions can form. Absorbers respond only to ES components compatible with their coupling.

State preparation can: - suppress certain components (e.g., filtering out momentum modes), - enhance others, - or entangle systems so that absorber states reflect joint absorber behaviour.

It changes the pattern of incipient transactions by forming them wherever ES–AS matches occur. State preparation determines:

  • which matches are possible,
  • their relative weights,
  • their global constraints (especially in entangled systems).

This is crucial because actualisation selects one incipient transaction via what Kastner calls spontaneous symmetry breaking. There is no explanatory mechanism for this “symmetry breaking” in PTQT, but the selection of the actual outcome is more analogous to random sampling. But there is no sampling mechanism. All there is, is a dispositional weighted set of tendencies for each possibletransaction and a stochastically selected outcome.

4.8.2 Why this matters for measurement and Bell correlations

Because state preparation determines the set of incipient transactions, it also determines:

  • the correlations that can appear,
  • the global constraints on entangled systems,
  • and the structure of possible actualisations.

This is why PTQT can explain Bell correlations without spacetime nonlocality:
the correlations arise from global constraints in the substratum, not from signals between spacetime events.

State preparation influences the quantum substratum in PTQT by altering the configuration of emission states through selection of a preferred absorber state via transactions in the pre‑spacetime substratum.

4.9 Conclusion

PTQT has a pre-spacetime, possibilist ontology in which:

  1. Quantum possibilities are physically meaningful
  2. Spacetime events are actualized transaction outcomes,
  3. Bell correlations arise from nonlocal possibilist constraints outside spacetime, and
  4. Relativistic structure is preserved by avoiding spacetime superluminal signaling.
